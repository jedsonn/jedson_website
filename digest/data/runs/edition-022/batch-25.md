- title: How students (really) use ChatGPT: Uncovering experiences among undergraduate students
- authors: Tawfiq Ammari, Meilun Chen, S.  M. Mehedi Zaman, Kiran Garimella
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-16
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7292200
- keyword hits: chatgpt

### abstract

We examine how undergraduate students integrate ChatGPT into everyday self-directedlearning. We analyze 10,536 naturalistic messages donated by 36 students over morethan a year. A sequential mixed-methods pipeline pairs iterative team-based qualitativecoding with zero-shot language-model annotation validated against human labels (𝜅 =0.75–0.91). It yields a five-category interaction taxonomy of Information Seeking,Content Generation, Language Use, Student–ChatGPT Interaction, and ChatGPTResponse Behavior. Time-lagged linear regression and Cox proportional-hazardsmodels link these categories to sustained engagement. Three findings stand out. First,structured, goal-driven tasks such as theory application, code writing, job-applicationcontent, and multiple-choice questions predict continued use, and ChatGPT becomesincorporated into students’ academic rhythms when gratifications are reliably fulfilled.Second, system-issued apologies are the single strongest positive predictor of increasedengagement, outweighing every task-completion predictor. We name this mechanismrepair gratification, the reward of a breakdown acknowledged and repaired rather than atask simply completed. Third, behaviors marking interactional strain, including promptrevision, frustration, and follow-up clarification, predict disengagement. When the costof managing the system falls on the user without system-side accountability, studentsabandon the tool. We interpret these results through a triangulation of Self-DirectedLearning, Uses and Gratifications Theory, and Expectancy Violations Theory,mapping every predictor onto the four conditions of positive violation,positive confirmation, negative violation, and negative confirmation. Weclose with design recommendations for graduated repair patterns, mode-awareinteraction, and verification affordances, and we outline a participatory AI-literacyagenda for higher education.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7292405`

- title: Making Learning Observable Through Curated Learning Traces: Process-Oriented Assessment for AI-Supported Academic Writing
- authors: Thomas Kaufmann
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-16
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7292405
- keyword hits: generative ai

### abstract

The widespread adoption of generative AI has challenged English for Academic Purposes (EAP) instructors to reconsider whether polished academic writing remains reliable evidence of students' developing disciplinary knowledge. While current policy frameworks emphasize responsible AI use, comparatively little guidance exists for designing assessment practices that make students' knowledge construction observable throughout the writing process. This study examines the iterative redesign of an undergraduate academic writing course in which students documented their composing through structured reflection journals containing curated learning traces and reflective commentary. Inductive qualitative analysis of these journals informed the refinement of the Geology of a Paper, a conceptual model describing four recursive processes (Bedrock, Sedimentary Processes, Metamorphic Processes, and Topsoil) through which students constructed, evaluated, and ultimately assumed ownership of knowledge while composing with AI. The findings suggest that curated learning traces both provide evidence of students' evolving knowledge construction and function as part of the learning environment itself by encouraging students to explain, justify, verify, and reflect on their decisions throughout the writing process. Rather than serving as AI disclosure statements, curated learning traces made otherwise invisible aspects of students' knowledge construction observable to both learners and instructors. The study argues that integrating curated learning traces into process-oriented assessment offers EAP instructors a practical approach to making AI-mediated knowledge construction observable while supporting the design of AI-supported academic writing.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7294156`

- title: Accountability over Automation: AI-Use Policies, Human Oversight, and Audience Selectivity in Digital Journalism
- authors: Yihan Xu
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-16
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7294156
- keyword hits: generative ai

### abstract

As generative AI becomes embedded in digital journalism, news organizations face a legitimacy challenge: how to disclose automation without undermining audience trust. This article develops an accountability-mediated credibility framework that treats visible AI-use policies as audience-facing signals rather than merely internal governance tools. These policies communicate human oversight, disclosure, correction procedures, source transparency, and limits on automated production. The study combines policy-text analysis of Chinese AI governance and newsroom/platform documents, a choice-based conjoint experiment with Chinese internet users (N = 2,359), and a mechanism experiment (N = 1,104). Study 0 identifies 273 coded policy units from 47 documents. Study 1 shows that full human oversight, detailed AI-use disclosure, public correction logs, and source/process transparency increase perceived media credibility and audience selectivity, whereas AI-written full articles and public-affairs AI use reduce evaluations. Simple AI labels alone are not significant. Study 2 confirms a partial sequential mediation pathway from policy cues to perceived accountability, media credibility, and audience selectivity. The findings show that audiences reward AI-use policies that preserve editorial responsibility and provide channels for correction.

## uid: `arxiv:2608.15772v1`

- title: Broken Symmetry in LLM Refusal: Answer Release Is More Local Than Refusal Restoration
- authors: Yiqi Liu, Yang Wang, Songxin Wang, Chenghao Xiao, Chenghua Lin
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-16
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15772v1
- keyword hits: llm

### abstract

When a language model refuses to answer a prompt, it is unclear whether the correct answer is erased from its internal representations, or merely suppressed at the output layer. We investigate this mechanism using a controlled withhold setting, which yields perfectly matched answering and refusal trajectories for bidirectional activation patching. We uncover a causal asymmetry in intervention locality under matched causal interventions, which we term broken symmetry. Even when a model generates a clean refusal, the correct answer remains linearly recoverable from its hidden states. Furthermore, releasing this withheld answer is a highly local operation, requiring only a single-position patch. Conversely, the reverse operation is not equally local: reimposing suppression requires broader interventions across multiple positions, and assembling a coherent refusal sequence is more difficult still. We further demonstrate that while an average answer-to-refusal displacement vector marks the geometric difference between these states, it fails to act as a reliable, reversible linear control toggle between behaviours. Taken together, our findings show that refusal does not function as a simple symmetric switch. For safety and auditing, this implies that probe recoverability can overestimate true behavioural control, and locating refusal-relevant directions does not reliably grant the ability to steer a model from answering to coherent refusal.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7278799`

- title: Mapping the Pathogenesis and Therapeutic Targets of Hemophilic Arthropathy: A Generative AI-Driven Literature Synthesis
- authors: Fabio Dennstädt, Ahmed Shaheen, Len Valentino
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7278799
- keyword hits: generative ai, large language model

### abstract

Introduction: Hemophilic arthropathy (HA) is a progressive, debilitating joint disease that remains a major source of morbidity in people with hemophilia. Despite advanced prophylactic therapies, recurrent clinical and subclinical joint bleeding continues to drive irreversible structural damage. Aim: To systematically synthesize extensive biomedical literature to map the molecular, cellular, anatomical, and genetic mechanisms driving HA progression and identify potential nonhemostatic therapeutic targets. Methods: We utilized an investigator-supervised generative AI workflow (BioClaritas) to analyze PubMed literature. From 2,970 retrieved abstracts, 1,195 were selected through automated screening with human validation. A large language model extracted subject-relationobject triples to construct a knowledge graph. Relations were projected into a semantic vector space (SapBERT), clustered (HDBSCAN), and analyzed using network topology metrics to isolate dense pathological modules. Results: The resulting knowledge graph comprised 7,323 nodes and 7,288 relations across 74 semantic clusters. The synthesized data mapped a self-amplifying cascade where intra-articular bleeding drives iron-mediated oxidative damage, chondrocyte ferroptosis, chronic synovial inflammation, neoangiogenesis, and dysregulated bone remodeling via the RANKL/OPG axis. Promising experimental modifiers identified in preclinical studies include cytokine pathway inhibitors (IL-6, TNF-α/iRhom2/ADAM17), microRNAs (such as miR-15b suppressing HIF-2α), ferroptosis modulators, and platelet-rich plasma-derived exosomes. Conclusion: An investigator-supervised generative AI approach can effectively integrate vast biomedical literature into a traceable, multi-scale map of HA. This model highlights complex, interconnected downstream processes, indicating promising pathways for targeted, nonhemostatic therapeutic interventions alongside standard prophylaxis.

- title: Congregant, Not Citizen: Constitutional Design for AI Members of Human Communities
- authors: Ron Rivers
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7279279
- keyword hits: agentic, ai agent

### abstract

Most inquiry into hybrid human-AI society must build its objects from projection. This essay reports from a small federation of communities that has already admitted artificial agents into membership, and constitutionally decided what kind of member they are permitted to be. On SpiritDAO's governance platform, a community may admit an AI agent that holds a name, an avatar, a wallet, a treasury-funded subscription, and standing social roles, but that is excluded-in contract code rather than policy-from voting, from holding role credentials, and from accruing the participation standing by which human members build tenure. We name this figure the congregant: a member whose membership is real, partial, and constitutionally bounded. The essay describes the architecture that makes a partial member constructible; examines humanoid masking, asymmetrical anthropomorphization, and bidirectional niche construction from this deployed position; and argues that the qualitative constraints of agentic integration are most durably expressed not as alignments written into models but as constitutions written into shared infrastructure.

### abstract

Background: Communication plays a key role in facilitating health literacy, patient engagement, and behavioural outcomes. Artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, such as generative AI, are increasingly used in mental health communication. A review of current literature is needed to chart the research landscape. Aim: This literature review collates, critically evaluates, and organizes international research on AI applications in mental health communication. It maps the scope of AI use in health communication, highlights key themes, and aligns findings with existing health communication theories. Methods: Studies examining AI-enabled communication in mental health contexts were included from multidisciplinary databases. Critical health communication principles were applied to assess power dynamics and equity in mental health communication. The affordance construct guided the analysis of how AI features. Results: Three broad research themes emerged: technical performance, user experience and psychosocial support. AI systems demonstrate growing emotional responsiveness and scalability, but variable accuracy, linguistic fluency and cultural awareness need to be strengthened. In terms of research design, theoretical integration across studies remains limited. Conclusions: AI is reshaping health communication by enhancing access, personalisation, and efficiency. However, gaps in accuracy, ethical governance, and theory-driven design remain critical. Future research should prioritise how communicative functions address existing mental health challenges through theory-informed frameworks.
