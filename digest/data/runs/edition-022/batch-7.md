- title: Standardized Statement of Account (SOA) API Architecture for RegTech & Automated Underwriting A Sovereign Infrastructure Blueprint for Real-Time Financial Supervision, Tax Verification, and Consent-driven Credit Assessment
- authors: Anup Brahma
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-19
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7293258
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Modern financial regulation and credit evaluation in emerging markets face structural inefficiencies due to reliance on periodic self-reporting, batch-processed data files, and manual sample audits. This working paper introduces a sovereign regulatory technology (RegTech) blueprint for a standardized Statement of Account (SOA) API framework. Designed to integrate seamlessly with India's Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)—specifically the Account Aggregator (AA) network and Unified Lending Interface (ULI)—this architecture transitions financial supervision from reactive compliance to continuous, 100% automated inspection. By coupling deterministic calculation engines with cognitive Large Language Models (LLMs), the framework addresses systemic risks such as delinquency evergreening, suspense account anomalies, and tax deduction misallocations. Furthermore, the paper operationalizes the legal dichotomy between statutory regulatory inspection powers (e.g., Section 35 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 and Section 17 DPDP Act exemptions) and commercial consent-driven underwriting. Standardizing granular, machine-readable SOA payloads empowers lenders to move beyond static, binary credit scores toward high-fidelity behavioral risk pricing.

- title: Multi-Agent Evaluation Framework for Government Affairs Large Language Models: A Case Study on Government Service Hotline Scenarios
- authors: Zengding Bai, Shuhui Ai, Qihang Gong, Dong Luo, Tianming Shao
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-19
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7315096
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Citizen service hotlines are large-scale citizen-administration interaction systems whose reliability and procedural compliance directly affect public trust. Embedding Large Language Models (LLMs) in such systems raises evaluation questions that static benchmarks and single-agent scoring leave unanswered, because they ignore the administrative logic and multi-stakeholder workflows that define routine operations. This study presents a Multi-Agent Evaluation Framework (MAEF) that audits government-affairs LLMs through three coupled mechanisms, namely compliance gatekeepers encoding domain constraints, heterogeneous agent collaboration simulating administrative roles, and a data-driven feedback loop that turns diagnostic findings into targeted optimization. A case study on intelligent work order distribution, using Yuhu District data from Xiangtan City, shows that a fine-tuned specialist model trades general alignment for domain precision. Coverage-risk curves then translate this trade-off into tiered deployment decisions suitable for grassroots government use. MAEF therefore validates individual models and guides risk-graded, auditable integration of LLM components into public operations systems.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7311198`

- title: The Crossroads of AI Social Decision: A Two-Dimensional Analysis of Decision Maturity and Cultural Orientation in LLMs
- authors: Xu Tang, Yifan Zeng
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-19
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7311198
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

As Large Language Models (LLMs) transition from passive text generators to autonomous social agents, decoding their underlying "cognitive DNA" is essential for ensuring alignment with diverse human values. This study investigates the intersection of socio-emotional maturity and cultural value orientations in frontier LLMs through the lens of cognitive science and cross-cultural psychology. We introduce the Dual-Axis Social Intelligence Scale (DASIS), a novel evaluative framework designed to decouple general social sophistication from implicit cultural biases-specifically along the Individualism-Collectivism (I-C) continuum. Across three interrelated experimental paradigms, we map the latent social architecture of nine state-of-the-art models. Study 1 establishes a behavioral baseline in Chinese, revealing that while models exhibit nearhuman social maturity, they suffer from a profound "Trans-Linguistic Value Locking" toward individualistic schemas, regardless of their developmental origin. Study 2 interrogates the "Whorfian Effect" (Linguistic Relativity) in synthetic cognition; findings indicate a Directional Asymmetry, where English context amplifies individualism while Chinese fails to exert a reciprocal collectivist pull, suggesting an "Axiomatic Individualism" ingrained during the alignment process. Study 3 explores "Cultural Perspective Taking," testing the models' Synthetic Theory of Mind (SToM) capabilities to voluntarily override default biases. Our results demonstrate that while default "personalities" are heavily skewed, models possess a latent Cognitive Decoupling capacity that allows for high-fidelity cultural simulation under explicit framing. This work provides a critical audit of the "WEIRD" (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic) bias in modern AI. We conclude that current alignment methodologies prioritize value consistency over cultural pluralism, potentially facilitating "Cognitive Imperialism" in global AI deployment. We propose DASIS as a standard for auditing the cross-cultural adaptability of autonomous agents.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7301618`

- title: Machine Self-Awareness: Functional Self-Modeling, Introspection and the Limits of Consciousness Claims in Large Language Models
- authors: Jemike Reid
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-19
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7301618
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Debates about machine consciousness often collapse several distinct questions into a single binary: is an artificial intelligence conscious or not? This paper argues that the binary framing obscures a set of increasingly measurable functional capacities in large language models (LLMs), including epistemic self-knowledge, behavioral self-modeling, situational awareness, introspective access to internal representations, and metacognitive control. Drawing on empirical work from 2022-2026, the paper proposes a Functional Self-Modeling Taxonomy that separates these capacities from phenomenal consciousness. Current evidence suggests that frontier models can sometimes estimate their own knowledge, identify learned behavioral tendencies, reason about their deployment context, and under controlled conditions report causally manipulated internal states. More recent interpretability work also suggests that verbalizable representations can occupy a privileged, globally accessible computational workspace. At the same time, these abilities remain unreliable, domain-specific, sensitive to post-training, and often fail to translate into robust selfregulation. The paper therefore rejects two symmetrical errors: treating every first-person report as evidence of consciousness, and treating all machine self-reference as mere imitation. Functional self-awareness can be scientifically meaningful without establishing subjective experience. This distinction matters for AI safety, interpretability, autonomy, model evaluation and future debates about machine moral status. The central claim is methodological: before asking whether a machine is conscious, researchers should specify which kind of selfknowledge, self-modeling or introspective access is being tested, how it is causally grounded, and whether it generalizes beyond the evaluation context.

- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7299985

### abstract

Context: Prompt engineering is now central to large language model (LLM) use, yet prompts are still commonly authored as opaque text artifacts that are difficult to validate, reuse, and maintain.Objectives: We introduce Prompt Engineering as Code (PEaC), a modular prompt-engineering approach implemented as a formally specified and executable framework, and evaluate whether it improves prompt expressiveness, reduces redundancy, and remains usable in practice. PEaC formalizes prompts as modular YAML specifications with inheritance, grammar-based validation, external-data providers, and graphical and programmatic interfaces.Methods: We evaluate PEaC through three complementary studies. First, we encode twelve established prompting strategies as PEaC modules to assess expressiveness. Second, we compare 210 non-modular and modular PEaC prompt pairs derived from a 215-prompt source collection based on “Awesome ChatGPT Prompts”. Third, we conduct a small-scale feasibility case study with a single professional dietitian across three synthetic patient cases.Results: Modularization reduces the deduplicated specification token count from 65,145 to 48,476 tokens, yielding a 25.59% token reduction ratio, 52.61% reuse efficiency, and a modularity index of 0.798, while preserving high semantic similarity between self-contained and modular prompts (cosine similarity = 0.954; BERTScore F1 = 0.930). The clinical case study should be interpreted as preliminary feasibility evidence rather than as a validated basis for broader claims of enterprise governance or professional adoption.

- title: ReguSim: Evaluating LLM Agent Rule Grounding in Financial Compliance
- authors: Yiyang Luo, Yihang Jiang, Qijun Xie, Liang Lan, Lin Willian Cong, Anyi Rao, Yunya Song
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-20
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19974v1
- keyword hits: deepseek, gemini, llm, llms

### abstract

LLM agents in financial markets may cite rules yet still submit orders that violate executable constraints or misread surveillance evidence. We introduce ReguSim, a controlled financial-compliance environment, and ReguBench, a target-marked monitoring benchmark, to separate four artifacts: stated reasoning, attempted action, execution enforcement, and monitor evidence. In trader runs with DeepSeek V4 Pro and Gemini 3.5 Flash, visible rules reduce but do not eliminate rejected actions, and incentive or persona framing shifts behavior. A bridge study shows that trader rationales can mislead an independent monitor unless enforcement evidence is shown. In monitoring, simple structured baselines either match or exceed prompt-only LLMs. The results frame financial compliance evaluation as an audit of rule-grounded actions and evidence use, rather than a single compliance score.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7327841`

- title: Human-Supervised AI-Assisted Knowledge Synthesis Framework for Hydrocyclone Design
- authors: Qiuzhe Hu, Ruizhi Jin, Kejun Dong, Richard Yang
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-21
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7327841
- keyword hits: gpt-5, large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Hydrocyclones are widely used for particle classification and solid–liquid separation in mineral processing and related industries. Although many empirical, mechanistic, numerical and data-driven models have been developed to predict hydrocyclone performance, their broader applicability remains limited by heterogeneous literature datasets, inconsistent parameter definitions and condition-dependent or contradictory reported trends. This study proposes a human-supervised AI-assisted framework, named SKADE (Synthesised Knowledge for AI-driven Design Engineering), to synthesise knowledge and integrate fragmented literature data and models. The framework integrates expert-defined ontology, large language models (LLMs), knowledge-informed data structuring, machine learning (ML) and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis to develop a knowledge-informed, data-supported and interpretable AI model for predicting hydrocyclone performance. To validate the framework, five highly cited hydrocyclone studies were analysed using GPT-5.5 and Codex under expert-defined extraction and reasoning rules. The extracted knowledge was manually validated to preserve contradictory findings and identify condition-dependent physical mechanisms. Based on the synthesised trends and conflicts, the literature dataset was organised using knowledge-informed structuring, significantly improving machine-learning prediction performance by reducing interference from contradictory and non-comparable relationships. Furthermore, SHAP independently validated that the framework captured distinct underlying physical mechanisms in different situations. The results demonstrate that SKADE provides a novel pathway not only for hydrocyclone modelling but also for cross-study knowledge integration and data-driven engineering design.
