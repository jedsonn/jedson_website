- title: LEGEND: A toolkit for differentially private graph publication using large language models
- authors: Shang Liu, Wei Zhang, Guan Yuan
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7309016
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models

### abstract

Differentially private graph publication releases a synthetic graph once and supports many downstream analyses, but existing methods drive a fixed stochastic model from coarse statistics, which bounds the structural detail they reproduce. We present LEGEND, the first framework to use a pre-trained large language model as the construction step. LEGEND summarises a graph by its degree sequence, perturbs it with Laplace noise satisfying ε-edge differential privacy, and encodes the sanitised summary into a structured prompt that guides the model to synthesise an edge list. Public metadata further improves fidelity at no privacy cost. Across four real-world networks, five privacy budgets and six graph queries, LEGEND consistently outperforms a state-of-the-art degree-based generator.

- title: Automated Anomaly Detection for Textual Commercial Contracts: BERT-Extracted Features and Fuzzy Neighborhood Rough Sets
- authors: Yanfang Ma, Zhongzhang Lv, Xiran Li
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7306025
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models

### abstract

Contract anomaly detection, as one of the fundamental building blocks for contract analysis, can identify potential violations and abnormal clauses efficiently, further help to reduce legal and operational risks and evaluate whether the contract complies with regulatory requirements and internal norms. Traditional contract detection relies on legal professionals and is plagued by problems such as low efficiency, high costs, and unstable detection quality. Consequently, automated contract detection has gained significant research attention. However, existing detection methods encounter three pivotal challenges. First, the named entity recognition model based on annotation suffers from inaccurate entity boundary detection. This leads to entity extraction errors and a decrease in the model's recognition performance. Second, contract data scarcity and expensive manual annotation hinder model training. Third, effectively eliciting knowledge from large language models for contract risk detection remains a challenge. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a multi-source feature fusion approach for contract anomaly detection. Four types of features are extracted from contract texts: semantic features, structural features, keyword features and TF-IDF features. These features are then concatenated and input into the Fuzzy Neighborhood Rough Set model to perform anomaly detection. We conducted experiments on 10 datasets, and 9 unsupervised algorithms are evaluated, including both established and contemporary anomaly detection techniques. The results demonstrate that BERT-FNRS achieves favorable performance. It performs prominently in Accuracy and ROCAUC, with average values of 0.9652 and 0.9252, respectively.

### abstract

This paper is a research note. Throughout the medieval period, a shōen (landed estate) known as Ōosato-shō existed in Kami District, Tosa Province. The institutional history of this shōen itself-the reconstruction of its documentary record, the identification of its place names, the realities of its local landholding class-has already been the subject of substantial, systematic survey work by regional historians. Building on those findings, this paper poses a different question: after the shōen dissolved as an institution over the course of the early modern period, why and how did its memory persist within household oral tradition? The material this paper examines consists of oral traditions preserved separately within the Kumon and Satomi families. The Kumon family preserves an account in which Hōjō Masako, in the Kamakura period, is said to have commissioned a certain Kumon (recorded in family tradition with the character zen) to establish a clinic or sanatorium in Kamakura during an epidemic, financing the undertaking against Ōosato-shō as collateral. This paper does not take a position on the historicity of these traditions. Empirical examination based on primary sources such as land survey registers is left to separate, ongoing research. This paper's concern lies in a single question: why do two independent family lineages each preserve the same proper noun-Hōjō-within their memory? - title: Empirically Grounded LLM Agents for Simulating Human Behavioral Adaptation During Urban Heatwaves: A Philadelphia Case Study
- authors: Yuqing Hu, Zexi Kuang
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7288363
- keyword hits: large language model, llm

### abstract

Urban heatwaves increasingly disrupt daily routines, activity timing, and access to critical resources, yet cities often lack tools for simulating how heterogeneous residents adjust behavior under extreme heat. Existing mobility and survey data can describe observed responses during past events, while conventional agent-based models often rely on fixed behavioral rules that may not capture context-sensitive adaptation. This study develops an empirically grounded large language model agent framework for simulating routine-constrained human behavioral adaptation during urban heatwaves. The framework integrates American Community Survey demographic profiles, American Time Use Survey daily routines, OpenStreetMap and SafeGraph spatial context, and LLM-based decision reasoning within an urban simulation environment. Using Philadelphia as a case study, we simulate routine summer conditions and a documented July 2024 heatwave and validate model outputs against ATUS-derived normal activity profiles and an independent heatwave household survey. Results show that empirical grounding substantially improves the reproduction of normal daily routines, increasing mean correlation with empirical activity profiles from 0.528 to 0.912 and reducing mean squared error from 0.066 to 0.008. Under heatwave conditions, the grounded model also better reproduces observed activity profiles, increasing mean correlation from 0.349 to 0.836 and reducing mean squared error from 0.098 to 0.012. These findings demonstrate that empirical demographic and time-use grounding can improve LLM-agent simulations of heatwave adaptation and provide a flexible tool for evaluating urban resilience strategies under realistic behavioral constraints.

- title: A Hybrid Fuzzy Bayesian Network with LLM-Assisted Knowledge Elicitation for Fault Detection and Diagnosis in Air Handling Units
- authors: Ziao Wang, Chujie Lu, Martín Mosteiro-Romero, Arjen Meijer, S. Walker, Laure Itard
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7309201
- keyword hits: large language model, llm

### abstract

Effective fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) is crucial for reducing HVAC energy consumption and supporting reliable building operation. Although recent data-driven methods have improved data efficiency and interpretability, their performance still depends on data that adequately represent the relevant faults and operating conditions. Knowledge-driven Diagnostic Bayesian Networks (DBNs) incorporate engineering knowledge into transparent probabilistic diagnosis, but their construction is affected by vague, subjective, and inconsistent expert judgments. This study develops a fuzzy set theory-based DBN (FST-DBN) framework for robust air handling unit (AHU) FDD using judgments from multiple human experts and a large language model (LLM) as an auxiliary virtual expert. The framework includes multi-source elicitation through a semi-structured questionnaire and a standardized LLM prompt, fuzzy-set-based DBN construction, and FDD implementation. It was validated using 11 labeled fault cases from a real-building AHU. The elicitation results showed substantial variation in fault occurrence frequencies and fault--symptom influence ratings, resulting in different prior probabilities, conditional probability tables, and retained network links. The best configuration, using objective-distance-based aggregation and pruning at $\tau=0.30$, correctly diagnosed 8 of the 11 cases (Top-1 accuracy = 72.7\%, Macro F1 = 0.67), compared with 7 cases (63.6\%, 0.61) for the best individual expert. Sensitivity analysis identified linguistic scale granularity and the pruning threshold as the most influential modeling choices, while confidence weighting had limited influence. The LLM matched the best-performing human expert but did not consistently improve the fused models.

- title: Brand Value Gravity Stop Reverse-Engineering Consumer Behavior Opening the Decision Black Box: G = M × VII × Te
- authors: Nicole Gao
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7282879
- keyword hits: gemini

### abstract

For three decades, brand-equity theory (Aaker, Keller, Kapferer) has rested on one assumption that was widely applied but never tested: that brand strength is the sum of its dimensional assets. The root of that assumption is methodological. The black box of consumer decision psychology resists forward disassembly, so analysts settled for the only route open to them and reverse-engineered brand strength from observed behavior. When several variables collapse at once (Nike Greater China, seven consecutive quarters of decline), reverse engineering can produce a diagnosis in which every line is correct and still leave the CEO with nowhere to start. This paper delivers a forward-engineering framework for opening that box: G = M × VII × Te. The equation reasons from the brand side. It measures what a brand, acting as a gravitational body, emits toward the consumer; which deep human instincts that emission activates; and how those instincts drive a consumer to spend willingly and to keep coming back. The three variables (Substance Redundancy, Vividness of Imagined Inhabitation, and the brand’s capacity to heat consumer emotion) each correspond to a separate dopamine reward pathway in the consumer’s brain. Each can bypass reason and act directly on the instinctive brain, releasing dopamine and driving the kind of purchase that ignores price and pulls the buyer back again and again. The multiplicative form means that any single variable falling to zero takes G to zero. The three are also causally asymmetric: M is the base. All repair has to begin at M, and marketing and M stand in a mathematically zero-sum relationship. The paper uses the synchronized collapse of all three variables in Nike Greater China as positive confirmation, and On Running’s countercyclical surge (full-price sales at a 62.8% gross margin) as the reverse anchor. It then sorts a company’s capacity to respond to the multiplicative trap into a four-tier spectrum, ranked by how precisely the company reads its own M: self-revolution (Titleist), instinct-driven escape (Gemini), cliff-edge escape (AMD), and stuck (Nike). Three diagnostic tools are provided for a CEO to use immediately.

### abstract

The rapid deployment of AI-native applications in complex wellbore operations requires unified, high-quality data integration to sustain continuous model evolution and reliable asset lifecycle management. However, heterogeneity in standards, semantics, and system architectures across legacy operational datasets limits their immediate applicability for dynamic and agile AI workflows. In alignment with the digital transformation priorities on information management, knowledge integration, and emerging technology implementations, this study develops a Human- and Entity-Centric Agentic Travelling Approach (HATA) for constructing dynamic-static ontology that support adaptive, cross-scenario data integration. This supports the energy transformation by enabling adaptive data integration for complex decision scenarios, validated through 3 typical types of downhole failure cases. HATA introduces a self-organising ontology construction framework composed of three specialised AI agents configured to address data quality challenges in knowledge modelling, and semantic enabled data integration. The planner interprets engineer-determined themes and decomposes them into ontology construction tasks; the traveller explores latent representational spaces to generate ontology candidates optimised for scenario relevance; and the supervisor assesses structural and semantic consistency, providing iterative feedback to guide refinement. The approach was evaluated using operational datasets (2021-2024) from 45 shale-oil wellbores in western China, focusing on wax deposition, scaling, and pump leakage, with corresponding financial management. More than 40 multi-modal parameters including deviation surveys, dynamometer cards, electrical parameters, inspection logs, operational and maintenance (O&M) alarms, and workover records were mapped through the HATA semantic and compared with expert curated benchmarks using a similarity threshold of 0.8 to quantify semantic alignment and structural completeness. Across all evaluated scenarios, the agentic ontology builder demonstrated substantial improvements in interpretability, operational efficiency, and data assembly performance. User intent comprehension achieved expect accuracy, with a single misinterpretation observed. Task planning precision approached optimal levels, while ontology construction cycles were completed within 40 seconds to 2 minutes, requiring minimal supervisory adjustments. Generated datasets reached an average semantic similarity exceeding 0.82 compared with field engineer benchmarks. Importantly, the resulting dynamic ontologies reduced decision-making time for failure diagnosis from approximately 1.5 hours to ≤10 minutes per case. These outcomes demonstrate the potential of HATA to benefit asset lifecycle management and strengthen AI-enabled operational workflows envisioned for wellbore intelligent transformation. This work introduces an innovative agentic travelling methodology for ontology construction, representing a novel innovation of genAI agentic system for ontology and knowledge modelling within shale oil wellbore downhole operations. Unlike static ontology, HATA also supports continuous evolution through digital-twin-driven updates, enabling the generation of adaptive “data blueprints” for predictive analytics. By bridging fragmented legacy datasets with emergent AI workflows, the methodology advances data science priorities in new technology deployment, valuation, and knowledge management, offering a scalable pathway for future energy transformation capabilities.
