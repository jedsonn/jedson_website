## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7276824`

- title: The AGI Transfer Test
- authors: Sahir Maharaj
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7276824
- keyword hits: foundation model

### abstract

General intelligence is usually assessed by the breadth and level of skills a system already possesses. That view can over-credit systems whose competence was purchased through pretraining, data coverage, or task-specific engineering. We study a stricter question: after an artificial system genuinely learns one skill, does that learning episode reduce the experience required to acquire a second skill in a domain that differs in surface semantics, observation modality, action space, interface, and ontology? We propose the AGI Transfer Test (ATT), a causal benchmark framework for measuring cross-domain skill-acquisition efficiency. ATT compares a source-trained branch against matched no-source and sham-exposure controls from the same pretrained checkpoint, then evaluates complete target-domain learning curves under fixed examples, compute, memory, tools, and optimization budgets. We introduce a five-dimensional domain-separation audit, a hidden-invariant construction principle, and a metric vector spanning zero-shot lift, threshold sample-efficiency ratio, normalized learning-curve gain, asymptotic transfer, negative-transfer burden, and source-skill retention. We synthesize evidence from meta-learning, task-transfer, generalist agents, cross-domain few-shot learning, embodied foundation models, ARC-style reasoning benchmarks, and 2026 agent-skill studies. Current evidence establishes that transfer can cross tasks, embodiments, and modalities, but it does not yet establish the strongest ATT claim: that learning an arbitrary skill causally accelerates learning in a strongly separated, previously held-out domain. The central conclusion is therefore conditional. A convincing AGI transfer result should not be defined by breadth alone, nor by zero-shot performance, but by reproducible reductions in the marginal cost of acquiring novel competence as domain distance increases, while controlling contamination, shared pretraining, forgetting, and negative transfer. ATT is intended as a falsifiable evaluation protocol for researchers studying learning-to-learn as a core property of increasingly general systems.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7302428`

- title: Giving Voice to Understanding: Interactive Oral Assessment as Dialogic Active Learning in Large, International Cohorts
- authors: Abdul Jaleel Razeed, Christian Russo, Ali Zaheer, Philip Le
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7302428
- keyword hits: generative artificial intelligence

### abstract

Business schools are being asked to assess judgement, communication and ethical reasoning in cohorts that are large, culturally diverse, and able to produce polished written work with generative artificial intelligence. This conceptual paper theorises interactive oral assessment as dialogic active learning and advances a model comprising four interlocking mechanisms: dialogics, agency, metacognition, and authenticity. Drawing on active learning, managerial thinking tasks, student voice, oral examination and assessment redesign, the model explains how structured conversation makes reasoning audible, distributes epistemic agency, opens assumptions to examination, and aligns assessment with the discourse of managerial work. The responsive follow-up question is identified as the pivot of the design, since unscripted probing separates rehearsed recall from reasoning constructed in the moment and protects prompt security in cohorts too large to examine in a single sitting. Written note-taking during the assessment is an equity affordance for students whose recall under pressure or confidence in speaking would otherwise constrain participation. A core postgraduate unit of approximately 1,500 students, about 95 per cent of them international, illustrates enactment at scale with calibration, moderation, and inclusive procedure. Nine design principles, a prompt taxonomy, a four phase implementation timeline and seven propositions translate the model into practice and research.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7276818`

- title: Robustness Over Efficiency: A Design Principle for Climate Policy Coalitions
- authors: Jürgen Renn
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7276818
- keyword hits: claude

### abstract

International climate cooperation has failed to build coalitions that include both energy importers and exporters because existing instruments offer exporters only exit, not adaptation. The failure is architectural rather than political: existing instruments rest on assumptions about institutional stability that international climate cooperation does not satisfy. Climate policy thus faces a design choice between maximizing allocative efficiency under idealized institutional conditions and accepting efficiency losses in exchange for robustness under fragile conditions. The paper develops the resulting trade-off as an explicit design axis between allocative efficiency and institutional robustness. Against the most sophisticated alternative at the efficiency end (the clean-up certificates of Lessmann, Gruner, Kalkuhl, and Edenhofer), it argues that current institutional conditions favour the robust instrument proposed here: a carbon currency system in which units of currency are required at the points where fossil carbon enters the economic system (upstream capture), are extinguished upon surrender rather than re-traded (destruction principle), and are governed as quantities rather than processes (technology-neutral regulation), opening an adaptation pathway also for petroleum-exporting states. Enforcement operates through market price differentials, and governance is disaggregated across four specialized institutions complemented by an intergenerational future damages reserve. The efficiency–robustness axis applies beyond climate policy since the design principle is general. ## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7276820`

- title: Agent-to-Agent Communication
- authors: Sahir Maharaj
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7276820
- keyword hits: llm

### abstract

Agent-to-agent communication is increasingly moving beyond the human-visible transcript. Classical multi-agent reinforcement learning allows agents to invent discrete symbols or continuous vectors; contemporary large-language-model systems increasingly explore embeddings, hidden states, and keyvalue caches as direct communication media. This paper asks a deceptively simple question: does removing human visibility cause AI systems to develop more efficient communication? We synthesize evidence from emergent-communication research, multi-agent LLM evaluations, latent-communication methods, monitorability studies, and steganographic-collusion work through 8 August 2026. We separate three concepts that are often conflated: representational visibility, or whether a message is humandecodable; access visibility, or whether humans can inspect the message; and temporal visibility, or whether oversight can inspect it before consequential action. The evidence supports a qualified answer. Removing a readability constraint enlarges the feasible protocol space and can enable lower-token, lowerlatency, or higher-fidelity channels. Yet opacity is neither necessary nor sufficient for efficiency. Unconstrained agents can learn maximally long, anti-efficient codes when message length is unpriced, while human-language grounding can preserve task performance and improve transfer to unseen partners. Recent latent-channel studies report substantial gains in selected settings, but some gains arise from systems-level cache reuse rather than from an emergent language, and 2026 results remain partly preprint evidence. We introduce VEC-4, a four-regime Visibility-Efficiency Communication framework, and A2A-VIS, a paired evaluation protocol that isolates channel visibility while measuring utility, communication cost, causal efficacy, transfer, monitorability, and security. The central conclusion is that the relevant frontier is not English versus 'AI gibberish.' It is efficiency versus auditability, interoperability, and control. For high-consequence agents, private machine-efficient payloads should be integrity-bound to visible commitments, independently verified, logged, and prevented from independently granting authority.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7300328`

- title: From Surface Deception to Cognitive Deception: Experimental Evidence on the Impact of Generative AI on Phishing Susceptibility
- authors: Muhammad  Adnan Haider
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7300328
- keyword hits: generative ai, generative artificial intelligence

### abstract

Phishing remains one of the most prevalent and costly forms of cybercrime, primarily because it exploits human decision-making rather than technical vulnerabilities. The emergence of generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) has transformed phishing by enabling attackers to produce highly persuasive, contextually relevant, and professionally written messages at scale. This study examines whether AI-generated phishing emails increase human susceptibility to cyber-attacks by enhancing perceived legitimacy, reducing suspicion, and amplifying urgency. Drawing on insights from phishing cognition, persuasion theory, and cybersecurity awareness research, the study compares user responses to legitimate emails, traditional human-written phishing emails, and AI-generated phishing emails. A quantitative experimental design was employed in which participants evaluated simulated email scenarios and reported their behavioral intentions. The findings indicate that AI-generated phishing emails are perceived as significantly more legitimate, less suspicious, and more urgent, and elicited stronger click intentions than traditional phishing messages. These findings suggest that generative AI is transforming phishing from a form of surface-level deception to one of cognitive deception and manipulation by exploiting trust, urgency, and routine decision-making processes. The study contributes to emerging scholarship on AI-enabled cyber threats by highlighting the growing importance of cognitive resilience and adaptive cybersecurity awareness strategies in defending against increasingly sophisticated social engineering attacks.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7283898`

- title: The Arguments We Stop Making
- authors: Sean Harrington
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7283898
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models

### abstract

The legal profession has spent three years worrying about the wrong problem. The fabricated citation is loud, binary, and already policed by the duty of candor; the sanctions docket proves the existing rules work. This Article is about the failure those rules cannot see. Randomized trials across cognitive science, education, and humancomputer interaction keep landing in the same place: large language models steer the people who use them. Users offload thinking and lose skill without feeling the loss. The tools agree too readily, out-persuade expert humans, and nudge different users toward the same output. Every commercial legal AI product runs on a base model tuned by the training step that research implicates. If the profession routes its reasoning through the same three or four tools, the body of legal argument will compress in ways no individual lawyer can detect. Briefs converge, the adversarial clash softens, and the raw material of common-law change (the odd distinction, the losing argument that later wins) thins out. The same tools now draft bills, comment letters, and testimony, so the compression reaches the lawmaking process itself, while price consolidates the market onto the cheapest base models, wherever they come from. The mechanism is established across disciplines; the legal-corpus evidence has not been gathered. This Article maps the research onto the tools lawyers use every day, tests it against the strongest objections on productivity and access to justice, and lays out the empirical study that would settle the question. The danger worth watching is not the argument the machine makes up. It is the argument that quietly stops getting made.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7280298`

- title: The Return of Intuitionism: Collapse and Redemption in the Age of Hyper-Complex Empires
- authors: Xuezhi Cheng
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7280298
- keyword hits: deepseek

### abstract

The evolution of modern civilization is accompanied by a profound epistemological illusion: the elevation of the "scientific method"-a localized tool of inquiry-into the ideology of "scientism," which purports to govern the totality of reality. This paper reveals how scientism's obsession with "absolute control" and actuarial logic pushes modern civilization toward a systemic crisis, crossing Joseph Tainter's threshold of diminishing marginal returns on complexity. The study highlights that modern society has constructed immense "defensive complexity" across financial hedging, regulatory compliance, and algorithmic governance. This attempt to execute "intertemporal hedging" against distant ecological and biological risks fundamentally violates evolutionary principles. When internal friction and administrative overhead exceed carrying capacity, the system descends into a hyper-fragile state of "brittle collapse." In the digital age, this pathological mechanism manifests as a symbiotic "digital feudalism" between the Washington political class and Silicon Valley tech oligarchs: Silicon Valley leverages geopolitical anxiety to package the energy-intensive brute-forcing of the Scaling Law as a monopolistic moat, using vast capital and physical entropy to mask algorithmic inefficiency while severely suppressing marginal, spontaneous innovation. By examining the algorithmic physics behind emerging phenomena like the open-source ecosystem and DeepSeek, this paper demonstrates that model capability is not a linear function of compute and capital; brute-force walls are far from insurmountable when confronted with radical architectural reconstruction. This not only shatters Silicon Valley's myth of monopoly but also exposes the profound truth that algorithmic breakthroughs stem from human adaptive intelligence and non-linear intuition. Ultimately, this paper proposes a path toward civilization's resilience: "De-complexification." The study emphasizes that overcoming this crisis requires not the design of higher-order actuarial models, but a comprehensive return to tacit knowledge, intuitionism, and embodied cognition. Only by breaking out of the self-entrapment of rational entropy and re-nesting abstract rationality within the biosphere and the continuous duration of life can civilization achieve ultimate redemption through evolutionary adaptation.

- authors: Lysiane Tendil
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-17
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7289964

### abstract

This article examines the European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) as reshaped by the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026, OJ L, 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026). The Omnibus operates as a selective recalibration of the AI Act: it postpones the standalone high-risk obligations of Annex III from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027, the product-integrated obligations of Annex I to 2 August 2028, and the operational deadline for national regulatory sandboxes under Article 57 from 2 August 2026 to 2 August 2027; it adds two new prohibited practices under Article 5 (non-consensual intimate imagery and AI-generated child sexual abuse material, applicable from 2 December 2026); it strengthens the AI Office's exclusive supervisory competence over GPAI-based AI systems; and it introduces simplification measures for SMEs and a newly defined category of small mid-cap enterprises. Six analytical dimensions structure the analysis: the regulatory architecture and Omnibus revision; compliance asymmetries; extraterritorial reach and the conditional operation of the Brussels Effect (Bradford, 2020); interaction with the intellectual property ecosystem; transatlantic and Asian regulatory competition; and voluntary compliance through the GPAI Code of Practice. The central thesis is that the Brussels Effect in AI operates conditionally and asymmetrically: Meta Llama 4 (April 2025) and Apple Siri AI (iOS 27, June 2026) illustrate partial market exit, while OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Anthropic have adopted adaptation strategies. This is the ninth article of an extended series and dialogues in particular with Article 7 (AI and industrial property) and Article 8 (AI hardware export controls).
