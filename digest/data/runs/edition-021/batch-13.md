## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7307251`

- title: Debt Prices the Opportunity Too: Generative AI in China’s Credit Market
- authors: Jifan Wang
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7307251
- keyword hits: chatgpt, generative ai, generative artificial intelligence

### abstract

Recent evidence from the United States indicates that equity markets price firms’generative artificial intelligence (AI) exposure as an opportunity while credit marketsprice it as a risk. We document that this asymmetry does not generalize. Usinga firm-level measure of workforce exposure to generative AI constructed from thetask content of pre-ChatGPT job postings, and a difference-in-differences design overa unified 2021–2025 event window, we find that the debt financing costs of highlyexposed Chinese listed firms decline following the release of ChatGPT. The impliedcost of debt, computed from financial statements for the full A-share universe, fallsby 11–13 basis points per standard deviation of exposure; primary-market bond issuance spreads fall by 7–10 basis points; and equity valuations rise. Exchange-tradedsecondary-market bond spreads, determined in a segment dominated by implicitlyguaranteed issuers, do not respond. The decline in financing costs occurs in theabsence of any observable increase in AI adoption and is concentrated among laborcost-intensive, financially unconstrained, non-state firms. The evidence is consistentwith creditors capitalizing an expected labor-cost-savings option for firms positionedas users, rather than targets, of the technology.

- title: Handshaking between Engineering and Picasso: Exploring New Horizons of LLM Defence through the Generation of Cubist Paintings
- authors: Biswajit Nath Roy, Wakar Yunus, Nehela Nasrin, Arijit Das, Diganta Saha
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7306257
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

AbstractRecent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) and text-to-image diffusion models have enabled high-quality image generation from natural language. However, existing systems still face challenges related to adversarial prompts, limited explainability, artistic controllability, and semantic preservation during stylization. This paper presents TrustArt, a unified framework for reliable and explainable Cubist image generation that integrates LLM-guided prompt intelligence, prompt safety verification, diffusion-based image synthesis, and the training-free GeoCubism transformation engine. The framework first refines and verifies the input prompt, generates a semantic base image using a diffusion model, and subsequently transforms it into a Cubist artwork throughpreprocessing, edge detection, geometric fragmentation, multi-perspective transformation, planar decomposition, palette simplification, and Cubist re-composition. The generated artworks are evaluated using SSIM, EPR, FD, VE, and PCR, together with Random Forest-based quality prediction and human perceptual evaluation. Experiments on 300 images achieve an SSIM of 0.82, EPR of 0.76, PCR of 0.71, FD of 0.64, and VE of 1.85. The Random Forest model achieves 81.0% accuracy and 0.87 AUROC, while human evaluation reports an overall MOS of 4.18/5. These results demonstrate the potential of TrustArt for producing semantically consistent, interpretable, and visually expressive Cubist artworks.

## uid: `arxiv:2608.18307v1`

- title: ComponentBench: Diagnosing Component-Level Failures in Computer-Use Agents
- authors: Tianchen Guan, Xinlei Lin, Royce Cheng-Yue, Xiangjun Wang, Shuyan Zhou
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18307v1
- keyword hits: gemini, gpt-5, qwen

### abstract

Current evaluation of computer-use agents is split between long-horizon workflow benchmarks and atomic GUI-grounding tests. This leaves an under-instrumented middle layer: realistic component-centered interactions (e.g., toggle a button set) that are short enough to diagnose and rich enough to capture the burdens of modern interfaces. We present ComponentBench, a benchmark and diagnostic pipeline for component-level evaluation of computer-use agents on modern web UIs. ComponentBench is organized around a library-agnostic ontology of 97 canonical UI components instantiated as 2,910 programmatically verified tasks across widely used component libraries, paired with cleaned human reference trajectories that enable evaluation of both task success and interaction efficiency. Beyond task collection, we introduce a scalable pipeline for auditing realized structural difficulty after implementation and synthesizing structured failure analyses across tasks and component families. Evaluating seven models -- GPT-5.4, Gemini 3 Flash, GPT-5.4 mini, GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, Qwen3-VL-235B, and UI-TARS-1.5-7B -- across four observation and action spaces, we show that these design choices critically impact performance. Within a single shared harness, changing only the observation and action space shifts task success by more than 30% for the same model: GPT-5 mini falls from 83.1% with accessibility-tree observations to 48.9% with coordinate-only Pixel control. Moreover, even the fastest configuration takes 3.7x as long as the matched human reference, and spatial manipulations that are trivial for humans continue to challenge current agents.

## uid: `arxiv:2608.17583v1`

- title: Auditing Exposure to Harmful Content on TikTok using Multimodal Language Models: A Cross-National, Age-Stratified Study
- authors: Hamidreza Saffari, Francesco Pierri
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-18
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17583v1
- keyword hits: gemini, llm, llms

### abstract

Online video platforms can expose young users to harmful content, but independent audits remain difficult because video annotation is costly and moderation judgments vary across languages. We audit TikTok in France, Italy, and Sweden with sockpuppet accounts representing four age personas (13, 16, 19, 40), collecting 36,971 videos from passive For-You-page scrolling and active sessions that scroll, search for harm keywords, and scroll again. To scale annotation, we validate four multimodal LLMs against native-speaker labels on a 300-video reference set. Gemini 2.5 Flash with eight sampled frames plus text performs best (aggregate kappa = 0.42), at half the per-call cost of native-video upload, and we apply it to a 10% sample for approximately \$50 in total API spend across both modalities. Keyword search returns 35-56% harmful content, a 1.5-7.5x increase over the scrolling baseline in ten of twelve country-age combinations; the spike is temporary and flattens the age differences observed in France and Sweden. Under passive scrolling, Italy has the highest harm rate at every age, with Italian age-19 reaching 48.6%. Overall, MLLM-based auditing offers a scalable approach for cross-national youth-safety audits, while provider safety filters (1.1% refusal rate) under-count the most explicit harms.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7315140`

- title: Quantifying representational mismatch between narratives and structured reports in near-death experiences
- authors: Cristian Pulido, Francisco Gómez, Prejaas K.B. Tewarie, Roxane S. Hoyer, Steven Laureys
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-19
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7315140
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Near-death experiences (NDEs) are often studied using both structured questionnaires and narrative reports, yet these representations cannot be assumed to provide interchangeable representations of the same experience. Narratives allow flexible expression of different aspects of the experience, whereas questionnaires organize reports into predefined dimensions and retrospective evaluations. How disagreement between these formats should be quantified and interpreted remains unclear. This study operationalized this disagreement as representational mismatch by comparing questionnaire-derived and narrative-derived labels across 190 NDE reports. Narratives were coded using 12 large language models (LLMs), complemented by human-reference annotation, a lexical baseline, external sentiment benchmarks, and preprocessing sensitivity analyses. Correspondence was evaluated across emotional tone, experiential features (NDE-C), and post-experience life changes (LCI-R).Agreement was systematic but incomplete and differed across representational domains. Experiential features showed stronger correspondence than life changes (NDE-C macro-F1 = 0.62 [0.60, 0.65] vs. LCI-R = 0.52 [0.50, 0.54]). Narrative tone contained meaningful affective information but corresponded only partially with global self-reported valence (macro-F1 = 0.43 [0.41, 0.44]), with disagreement concentrated mainly in mixed and neutral narrative-tone assignments rather than direct polarity reversals. Standard sentiment benchmarks showed substantially higher correspondence (macro-F1 = 0.78 ± 0.16), making a purely general model-limitation explanation less plausible.These findings support interpreting cross-format disagreement as a structured property of reporting rather than measurement or extraction error alone. Representational mismatch provides a framework for identifying where different reporting formats converge, where they cease to be interchangeable, and how the elicitation format shapes what becomes observable in research on subjective experience.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7310878`

- title: The LLM Effect: AI Exposure and Wage Growth Across U.S. Occupations Since 2019/2022
- authors: Thomas Soliman
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-19
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7310878
- keyword hits: chatgpt, large language model, llm

### abstract

This paper tests whether U.S. occupations with higher AI exposure experienced different wage growth since ChatGPT's 2022 release and before the ChatGPT era (2019). Using an original exposure score built from a large language model's task level self-assessments across the top 100 U.S. News-ranked occupations matched to BLS wage data from 2019-2025. We found a statistically significant negative relationship between AI exposure and wage growth: more AI-exposed occupations grew more slowly. However, this relationship holds similarly in the years before ChatGPT existed as after, which might suggest it reflects a longstanding wage growth pattern rather than a ChatGPT-specific effect. These findings contribute one of the first empirical tests of AI exposure against post-ChatGPT U.S. wage data, while highlighting the difficulty of isolating AI's causal labor market effects from pre-existing trends.

- title: BanglaCompound-HW: A Handwritten Bangla Compound Character Image Dataset for OCR and Deep Learning Research
- authors: Srabonti Rozario, Abir Mahmud Shahariar, Sanjana Islam Kasfia, Md. Ismiel Hossen Abir
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-19
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7308559
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Bangla is one of the most spoken languages in the world. However, handwritten Bangla character recognition is challenging because the script contains many compound characters. These compound characters often have similar shapes, complex structures, and different writing patterns. Therefore, it is challenging for Large Language Models (LLMs) to identify characters accurately. To solve the compound character-related issues, we develop the Bangla Compound Handwritten dataset. The dataset contains approximately 3,000 images from 30 commonly used Bangla compound character classes. Around 100 handwritten samples were collected for each class from 10 volunteer contributors with different handwriting styles. The dataset has a balanced class distribution and contains approximately 100 images for each class. It also contains natural variations in handwriting, which can help improve model generalization. Researchers can use this dataset for character classification, OCR benchmarking, image classification, and deep learning experiments. The dataset is publicly available through Mendeley Data.
