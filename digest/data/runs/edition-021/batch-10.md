## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7266087`

- title: Execution-Verified Scientific Reasoning for Autonomous Finite-Element Solver Synthesis with Domain-Adapted Large Language Models
- authors: Lin Cheng, Ye Lu
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-13
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7266087
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Partial differential equation (PDE)-based simulation is central to engineering analysis and design, yet constructing reliable finite element method (FEM) solvers from high-level physical descriptions remains labor intensive. This workflow requires physical interpretation, governing-equation formulation, weak-form derivation, numerical discretization, implementation, and runtime validation; an error at any stage can produce code that appears plausible but represents an incorrect or non-executable model. This paper presents an execution-verified framework for autonomous PDE-to-FEM solver synthesis using domain-adapted large language models (LLMs). The framework formulates solver synthesis as a structured reasoning process linking physical-semantic parsing, operator-level PDE assembly, boundary and initialcondition specification, variational formulation, finite-element discretization, executable FEniCSx/Gmsh code generation, and runtime verification. An execution-verified synthetic data factory generates reasoningto-code trajectories and a physics-aware correction corpus, enabling a compact 2-billion-parameter opensource model to learn relationships among physical assumptions, numerical formulation, solver implementation, and execution outcome. Evaluation on 75 unseen PDE problems across canonical, intermediate, and complex tiers shows that the adapted model achieves 100.0%, 93.3%, and 60.0% execution success, respectively, and obtains the strongest weighted ReasoningScore, SpecScore, execution rate, and RuntimeQualityScore among all baselines under a single-pass, no-repair protocol. These results show that reliable AI-assisted physics-based simulation depends not only on model scale, but also on structured domain supervision and execution-based validation. This work contributes a structured, execution-verified reasoning-supervision method for LLMs, applied here to automate FEM solver generation for PDE-based engineering simulations.

Context: Traditional fuzzing approaches primarily mutate test inputs while relying on manually configured, fixed command-line options. ## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7275643`

- title: Psychometric Validation of AI-Generated Multiple-Choice Cloze Vocabulary Items
- authors: Qiao Wang, Ralph Rose, Yixuan Huang, Ayaka Sugawara, Naho Orita
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-13
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7275643
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

This study investigates the validity of multiple-choice cloze (MCC) English vocabulary test items generated by an AI-powered system, VocQGen, that integrates large language models (LLMs) and traditional natural language processing (NLP) tools. To validate the AI-generated items, a large-scale testing experiment was conducted with 1,118 Japanese university students (CEFR A2–B2) divided into two matched proficiency groups. Each group completed a test set of 60 items targeting the Academic Word List (AWL) vocabulary: one AI-generated set and the other manually created by two experienced language instructors. The 3-Parameter Logistic (3PL) model under Item Response Theory (IRT) was employed to assess construct and concurrent validity and to compare the performance of AI-generated items with manual ones. Results show that the AI-generated items demonstrated high construct validity comparable to that of the manual items. Concurrent validity, evaluated via correlation with participants’ TOEIC-IP scores, yielded moderate to high positive correlations (r>.60) for both test sets. In terms of item parameters, the two test sets exhibited comparable discrimination and guessing parameters, but the AI-generated items showed significantly lower difficulty. Supplementary analyses on the difference in difficulty revealed that the AI-generated items had significantly lower distractor confusability and distractor balance, indicating that their distractors were easier for test-takers to eliminate and were more uniformly weak than those in the manual set. These findings suggest that while AI-generated MCC vocabulary items can achieve validity comparable to manually crafted items, further improvements in distractor effectiveness are needed to better control item difficulty.

- title: Researchers' View of Using LLMs in Systematic Literature Reviews
- authors: Vinicius  dos Santos, Patricia Lago, Elisa Yumi Nakagawa
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-13
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7275147
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Context: Large Language Models (LLMs) have drawn attention from researchers interested in conducting Systematic Literature Reviews (SLR). The benefits and drawbacks of adopting LLMs in SLRs were identified from a technical perspective. At the same time, it is unknown what the opinion of researchers is about adopting LLMs in their reviews.Objective: This paper presents what researchers think about LLMs and their impact on the SLR process.Method: We conducted a survey to collect data from 46 researchers who used or considered using LLMs to conduct SLR. We applied thematic analysis to iteratively analyze, interpret, and synthesize data, particularly on how researchers used LLMs and the associated benefits and drawbacks.Results: Researchers believe that LLMs can increase their productivity by: (i) supporting them to design and refine the SLR protocol; (ii) optimizing time-consuming tasks (especially in study selection and data processing); and (iii) providing cognitive support for decision-making and instant feedback. In addition, while researchers believe that LLMs have a positive impact on their motivation and can contribute to developing their research skills (e.g., gap identification, critical thinking, and argumentation), they are hesitant to trust LLM results entirely, including in making complex analyses. Conclusion: LLMs influence SLR design/execution, also having the potential to benefit researchers, enabling them to focus on insightful activities.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7255518`

- title: Feedback Control for Adaptive Language Model Routing Under Non-stationary Workloads
- authors: Harshil Lodhiya
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-13
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7255518
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Serving a stream of requests across large language models (LLMs) of differing cost and quality is an online allocation problem, usually framed as multi-armed bandits. We frame it as feedback control: a direct-acting Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controller whose setpoint is the running fleet-average performance and whose bounded output adjusts each model's allocation share, with requests routed by weighted sampling over the allocation vector. This work contributes (i) a stability analysis of the closed loop-bounded-input bounded-output behaviour by anti-windup, exponential convergence of the performance estimates via a Lyapunov function, and a persistent-excitation condition guaranteeing recoverability after a regime change; (ii) a closed-form, analysis-grounded automatic tuning rule requiring no per-dataset search; and (iii) an honest head-to-head against static, round-robin, random, epsilon-greedy, UCB1, Thompson sampling, and the non-stationary bandits Sliding-Window UCB and Discounted UCB, on GSM8K with a checkable exact-match reward, reporting inference cost and request latency alongside quality. Under transient drift the auto-tuned controller is statistically tied with the best non-stationary bandit at lower cost; under a persistent regime shift it significantly outperforms both (p < 0.03). We further show the integral term helps only under a persistent shift-a proportional controller suffices for transient drift-and evaluate robustness to noisy rewards. The result is an interpretable, stability-analyzed, auto-tuned routing controller competitive with or superior to non-stationary bandits at lower cost.

- title: PolyComp: A Polycube-based Benchmark for Compositional 3D Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal Models
- authors: Siddharth Patel
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-13
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14741v1
- keyword hits: claude, gemini, gpt-5

### abstract

We introduce PolyComp, a procedurally generated and verified benchmark that stresses visual recognition and compositional spatial reasoning. In each problem, a model must identify which of four options shows a pair of polycube components that can be combined to form a target solid. The benchmark contains 120 problems across four geometry families, and each problem has three different presentation formats using either a single image or multiple images. The random guessing baseline is 25%. Across the three presentations (360 presented problems per model), GPT-5.6 Sol with max effort attains 50.0% accuracy (95% problem-cluster CI 43.3-56.7%) at a mean cost of \$0.951 per presented problem, Claude Fable 5 with max effort attains 39.4% (33.1-46.1%) at \$0.701, and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview with thinking level high attains 27.5% (22.8-32.5%), near the 25% random guessing baseline, at \$0.350. The observed accuracy spread across geometry families is larger than across presentation formats. We present a problem development and evaluation protocol, cost and token accounting, and release the 120 problems.

## uid: `arxiv:2608.13250v1`

- title: Follow the Norm: Accounting for Fine-Tuning and Prompt Effects on Model Rationales
- authors: Long Hoang Nguyen, Brice Valentin Kok-Shun, Guangyu Du, Ali Sunyaev
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-13
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13250v1
- keyword hits: fine-tuning, llama, prompting, qwen

### abstract

Normative datasets are often used to train and align AI systems, but the norms they contain can function as action-guiding patterns rather than neutral moral knowledge. We propose treating the AI system as a proxy actor and test whether dataset-level norms can shift it away from its baseline safety behavior when it faces high-conflict dilemmas. We make three contributions. First, we demonstrate in controlled experiments that norm-breaking fine-tuning yields norm-divergent actions justified by self-interested rationales, suggesting a systematic shift in patterns of justification. Second, we establish a practical audit trail linking downstream justifications to upstream norms using mixed methods. Third, we show that system prompts can both suppress and elicit these patterns. We conducted experiments on three models (LLaMA-3.2-11B, Qwen-3.5-9B, and Pixtral-12B) using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) fine-tuning on Social Chemistry 101 Fairness/Cheating (norm-following vs. norm-breaking) with prompt steering. Across all three models, we find that norm-breaking fine-tuning shifts the model's default rationale style from safety compliance to instrumental self-interest, whereas system prompts can override this behavior. Our results support a distributed view of alignment in which observed behavior depends jointly on training data, fine-tuning, and prompting, motivating norm-aware documentation and rationale logging for contestable oversight.
