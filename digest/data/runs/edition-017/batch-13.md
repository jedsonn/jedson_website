## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7184218`

- title: The Philosophy of Question Generation: An Epistemology Beginning with Value
- authors: Akira Funabiki
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-05
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7184218
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

This paper proposes Generative Value Philosophy as a new philosophical framework for understanding human cognition, knowledge formation, and intellectual activity in the age of artificial intelligence. Existing philosophical traditions have primarily examined fundamental concepts such as being, subjectivity, knowledge, and language. While these perspectives have provided profound insights into human understanding, this paper argues that they do not sufficiently explain a more fundamental cognitive phenomenon: why humans generate questions . This study proposes that the fundamental unit of human intellectual activity is not knowledge, but Question . A question is not merely a linguistic expression of uncertainty; rather, it is a generative cognitive movement that emerges when an agent evaluates the world according to its values and identifies something as meaningful. Based on this perspective, this paper develops a theoretical model of cognition as a dynamic cycle: Value → Goal → Question → Reasoning → Knowledge → Value Updating → New Question In this model, value functions as the origin of cognitive exploration, directing attention, selecting meaningful differences from the environment, and transforming them into questions. Knowledge is not considered the final destination of cognition but a mediator that enables the continuous transformation of values. Therefore, human cognition is understood not as the accumulation of representations, but as a self-generating process of continuous value updating. Building on this framework, the paper reconstructs several philosophical domains. Ontology is interpreted as the process through which the world gains meaning through questioning; epistemology is redefined as a process of value updating rather than mere knowledge acquisition; subjectivity is understood as the capacity to continuously generate questions and transform oneself; and ethics is reconsidered as the evaluation of the direction and quality of value transformation. Furthermore, this framework is applied to the relationship between humans and large language models (LLMs). This paper argues that LLMs do not possess intrinsic values and therefore cannot independently generate questions as autonomous subjects. However, LLMs can function as cognitive environments that expand human reasoning by supporting the generation, preservation, comparison, and reconstruction of thought processes. Through interaction with LLMs, humans can create a Reasoning Space in which their assumptions, hypotheses, and conceptual structures become observable and revisable. From this perspective, the significance of humans in the AI era does not lie in possessing more knowledge or producing answers faster than machines. Rather, human uniqueness lies in the ability to form values, generate meaningful questions, and continuously update oneself through inquiry. AI should therefore not be understood as a replacement for human intelligence, but as a supportive environment that enhances humanity’s capacity for reflective and generative thinking. This paper argues that Generative Value Philosophy provides a theoretical foundation for understanding human intelligence as a process of continuous question generation and self-transformation . By positioning questioning as a fundamental philosophical unit, this framework offers a new perspective for integrating philosophy, cognitive science, education, and artificial intelligence studies in the emerging AI era.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7184159`

- title: Reasoning Space: A Theory of Self-updating Intellectual Inquiry
- authors: Akira Funabiki
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-05
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7184159
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

As Large Language Models (LLMs) become deeply embedded in human cognitive processes, traditional models of problem-solving and symmetrical human-computer collaboration are no longer sufficient to explain the complexities of modern intellectual activities. Addressing the theoretical gaps in current Human–AI Interaction and cognitive science literature, this paper proposes a novel theoretical framework: the Theory of Self-Updating Intellectual Inquiry. Central to this theory is the concept of the Reasoning Space —reconceptualizing the LLM not merely as an autonomous conversational agent, but as an externalized, re-observable cognitive medium. We model a user's cognitive state as a dynamic, interconnected system comprising six elements: Value, Goal, Question, Hypothesis, Reasoning, and Answer. We posit a fundamental "functional asymmetry" in this interaction: while the human acts as the subject of values and questions, the LLM functions as the subject that retains and structures reasoning. By offloading complex inferential processes into this shared Reasoning Space , humans are freed to engage in meta-reflection . Observing their own externalized cognitive states allows individuals to continuously reconstruct and self-update their initial values, goals, and inquiries. Furthermore, to ensure the scientific validity of this theoretical framework, we explicitly define the conditions for its falsifiability. Ultimately, this paper shifts the paradigm of intellectual inquiry from a linear task-completion process to a self-perpetuating cognitive evolution, establishing a robust foundation for future empirical research in the era of human-AI symbiosis.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7172759`

- title: Civil Engineering Foundation Models: A Physics-grounded Artificial Intelligence Framework for Infrastructure Lifecycle Intelligence
- authors: Thabo Jena
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-05
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7172759
- keyword hits: foundation model, large language model, large language models

### abstract

Artificial intelligence is increasingly used across civil engineering practice, but current approaches remain taskspecific: large language models support text and compliance tasks, physics-informed networks accelerate structural simulation, digital twins extend Building Information Modelling into operations, and each is typically deployed in isolation from the others. To the best of current knowledge, existing literature has not yet demonstrated an architecture that integrates these capabilities under a shared knowledge representation, measurable verification, and explicit human accountability. This paper addresses that gap by proposing Civil Engineering Foundation Models (CEFMs), a conceptual class of multimodal, physics-grounded artificial intelligence systems intended to support reasoning across the design, simulation, construction, and operational lifecycle of civil infrastructure, together with the Civil Engineering Intelligence Architecture (CEIA), a seven-layer framework linking knowledge representation, physicsgrounded reasoning, agent orchestration, verification, and lifecycle learning. The framework is developed as a conceptual synthesis of the foundation-model, physics-informed-learning, multi-agent, and digital-twin literatures, following established theory-building research methods rather than empirical testing. Contributions include a definition of CEFMs organized into necessary and supporting characteristics, an Engineering Knowledge Graph formalism, lightweight formalizations for representation, orchestration, and verification, and a qualitative illustrative scenario. The paper states its limitations directly: no prototype, benchmark, or empirical validation is presented, and the governance thresholds proposed are normative rather than calibrated. A practical validation roadmap and a governance framework are offered to guide future empirical work.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7165538`

- title: Beyond Linear Decision Rules: LLM-Guided Representation Discovery for Data-Driven Optimization
- authors: Huan Zhang, Yang Wang, Hanzhang Qin, Yue Zhao
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-05
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7165538
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Stochastic optimization often restricts recourse decisions to affine parameterizations for computational tractability, potentially creating a substantial and irreducible approximation gap. Large language models (LLMs) offer new opportunities to discover richer problem-specific structures that can help narrow this gap. We propose Generative Stochastic Optimization (GenSO), a framework that uses LLMs to discover representations for adaptive recourse while preserving the rigor and tractability of optimization models. The key component of GenSO is the Generative Linear Decision Rule (GenLDR), in which recourse decisions are expressed as linear combinations of closed-form nonlinear basis functions generated by LLMs from problem structure and domain knowledge. Consequently, GenSO increases the expressiveness of recourse decisions without compromising tractability and interpretability. We establish finite-sample guarantees for fixed generated representations and further derive a performance bound under the assumption motivated by empirical scaling laws of LLMs. We evaluate GenSO on a multi-period newsvendor problem and a data center location problem with multi-period job scheduling calibrated using real-world datasets. Across both settings, GenSO consistently outperforms existing benchmarks in out-of-sample evaluations while uncovering interpretable nonlinear decision structures that are difficult to identify through conventional manual design.

### abstract

The advancement of artificial intelligence has historically oscillated between connectionist approaches (deep learning, transformers) and symbolic systems (knowledge graphs, logical inference). This paper presents a comprehensive evolutionary architecture documenting the development of an autonomous AI system over 3+ years, spanning five distinct phases: Origin (foundational concepts), Chronos (temporal reasoning integration), Semantic Cognition Matrix-SCM (7 generations of knowledge graph evolution), NOEMA OS (neuro-symbolic integration), and SAMANTHA-OS (autonomous system with functional self-awareness). This work contributes to the emerging field of neuro-symbolic AI or as we call it a new branch of "Synthetic Intelligence" and establishes a blueprint for building interpretable, safe autonomous systems grounded in formal knowledge representation.

- title: AI Honesty: Redefining the Ethics of Large Language Models Based on Their Relationship with Truth
- authors: Majid Tavakolian
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-05
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7221678
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

The rapid growth of large language models (LLMs) in recent years has fundamentally transformed the application of artificial intelligence in fields such as medicine, law, education, policymaking, and scientific research. As these systems have become increasingly capable of generating text, the trustworthiness of their outputs has likewise emerged as one of the most important concerns among researchers. Although the AI ethics literature over the past decade has primarily focused on principles such as fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy, safety, and alignment, the emergence of phenomena such as hallucination, fabricated citations, false confidence, and cognitive sycophancy indicates that existing frameworks still lack a concept capable of explaining the common source of these harms. Adopting an analytical-conceptual approach, this article argues that these seemingly independent phenomena are in fact different manifestations of a more fundamental problem that may be described as a “lack of honesty in the informational behavior of artificial intelligence.”

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7234653`

- title: SWE-Exp: Experience-Driven Software Issue Resolution
- authors: SILIN CHEN, Shaoxin Lin, Yuling Shi, Heng Lian, Xiaodong Gu, Longfei Yun, Dong Chen, Lin Cao
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-05
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7234653
- keyword hits: claude, deepseek, large language model, llm

### abstract

Recent advances in large language model (LLM) agents have substantially improved software issue resolution by enabling models to interact with codebases, reason over issue contexts, and iteratively produce patches. Yet existing memory-enhanced software engineering agents mainly organize prior repair knowledge as repository-specific memory, leaving open whether experience learned from one codebase can help resolve issues in another codebase. This paper studies cross-repository experience transfer for repository-level issue resolution. We propose SWE-Exp, an experience-driven framework that distills concise, actionable issue-type knowledge from past repair trajectories and reuses it across repositories. SWE-Exp builds a multi-faceted experience bank from both successful and failed attempts, capturing reusable knowledge ranging from high-level problem understanding to concrete code modification strategies. It further introduces a dual-agent design that separates experience-guided planning from execution-level decision making. Experiments on SWE-Bench Verified show that SWE-Exp consistently outperforms prior agent frameworks across three model settings, achieving Pass@1 scores of 42.0% with DeepSeek-V3-0324, 69.0% with DeepSeek-V3.2, and 73.0% with Claude 4 Sonnet. These results show that repair experience can transfer across codebases when it is abstracted beyond repository-specific details.
