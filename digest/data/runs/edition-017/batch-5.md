## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.4910800`

- title: From Feature Importance to Natural Language Explanations Using LLMs with RAG
- authors: Sule Tekkesinoglu, Lars Kunze
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2024-08-12
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4910800
- keyword hits: foundation model, large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

As machine learning becomes increasingly integral to autonomous decision-making processes involving human interaction, the necessity of comprehending the model's outputs through conversational means increases. Most recently, foundation models are being explored for their potential as post hoc explainers, providing a pathway to elucidate the decision-making mechanisms of predictive models. In this work, we introduce traceable question-answering, leveraging an external knowledge repository to inform the responses of Large Language Models (LLMs) to user queries within a scene understanding task. This knowledge repository comprises contextual details regarding the model's output, containing highlevel features, feature importance, and alternative probabilities. We employ subtractive counterfactual reasoning to compute feature importance, a method that entails analysing output variations resulting from decomposing semantic features. Furthermore, to maintain a seamless conversational flow, we integrate four key characteristics-social, causal, selective, and contrastive-drawn from social science research on human explanations into a single-shot prompt, guiding the response generation process. Our evaluation demonstrates that explanations generated by the LLMs encompassed these elements, indicating its potential to bridge the gap between complex model outputs and natural language expressions.

- title: A Systematic Survey of Self-Evolving Agents: From Model-Centric to Environment-Driven Co-Evolution
- authors: Zhishang Xiang, Chengyi Yang, Zerui Chen, Zhimin Wei, Yunbo Tang, Zongpei Teng, Zexi Peng, Zongxia Li
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-05-05
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6626878
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has empowered autonomous agents with advanced reasoning, planning, and tool-use capabilities. However, traditional agent systems rely heavily on human-guided training. Despite the reduced data volume in post-training stages, the increasing demand for high-quality supervision creates a critical scalability bottleneck. This dependency not only restricts scalability but also confines agents to the upper bound of human expertise, severely limiting their evolutionary potential. To overcome these limitations, research on autonomous agents is shifting towards the Self-Evolving Agents paradigm, in which agents autonomously coordinate their own improvement loops with minimal human supervision. To unify this rapidly expanding field, we propose a taxonomy that categorizes methods into three core components of an autonomous evolutionary cycle: (i) Model-Centric Self-Evolution, where agents enhance internal capabilities through inference scaling or parameter bootstrapping; (ii) Environment-Centric Self-Evolution, where agents achieve continual self-evolution by interacting with the environment to obtain external knowledge and experience-based feedback; and (iii) Model-Environment Co-Evolution, where agents and their environments jointly evolve through sustained interaction. We highlight Model-Environment Co-Evolution as a key emerging direction for Self-Evolving Agents, where environment definitions and their co-evolution with agents will become central challenges in future research. We provide a systematic technical foundation of Self-Evolving Agents, current implementations, and identify key technical challenges and promising research directions. All related resources, including research papers, open-source data, and projects, are collected for the community in https: //github.com/XMUDeepLIT/Awesome-Self-Evolving-Agents.

- title: Trustworthy Recommendation in the Era of Large Language Models: Opportunities and Challenges
- authors: Bohao Wang, Yu Cui, Zhenxiang Xu, Jujia Zhao, Chenxiao Fan, Jizhi Zhang, Weiqin Yang, Shengjia Zhang
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-07-29
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6832362
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

The field of recommender systems (RS) is currently undergoing two profound paradigm shifts. From the perspective of objectives, the goal has shifted beyond mere recommendation accuracy to comprehensive trustworthiness, encompassing multiple dimensions such as robustness, fairness, and privacy preservation. From a technical perspective, Large Language Models (LLMs) have been extensively integrated into RS, reshaping the foundations of recommendation through richer semantic understanding, stronger intent reasoning, and more flexible user interactions. The convergence of these two shifts prompts a timely and pivotal question: how does the integration of LLMs reshape the landscape of trustworthy recommendation? In this work, we present a systematic review of trustworthy LLM-empowered recommendation. By comprehensively analyzing over 200 recent studies, we reveal that the introduction of LLMs acts as a double-edged sword. While their advanced mechanisms and user-friendly interfaces offer unprecedented opportunities to enhance trustworthiness, they simultaneously introduce new risks, such as novel forms of bias and hallucination-induced issues. To characterize this dual impact, we systematically identify 13 opportunities and 18 challenges across six fundamental dimensions of trustworthiness, and accordingly organize the existing literature into a novel taxonomy. We also provide a comprehensive review of commonly used datasets and evaluation metrics to facilitate empirical validation. Finally, we identify critical open challenges and outline future directions, hoping to inspire future research on this emerging topic.

- authors: Siqi Wei, Suzy Xia, Mengshuo Jia, Bo Yang
- affiliations: not stated

- source: SSRN

### abstract

Energy management for fuel-cell–battery autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) is highly mission-dependent. Existing controllers are largely designed and tuned by experts for specific conditions. Thismanual process explores only a limited design space and cannot reliably identify a high-performingcontroller when mission requirements change. Large language models (LLMs) provide a possibleroute to automated controller design, but they lack AUV-specific knowledge and cannot verifywhether a proposed controller is executable or effective. To bridge this gap, this paper proposes aKnowledge-Constrained Agent Controller Generation (KACG) framework. An expert knowledge basefirst defines the admissible design space through registered models, control modules, compatibilityrules, and parameter bounds. An agent-compatible electrothermal platform then instantiates candidatecontrollers and evaluates them through deterministic checks and closed-loop simulation. A six-agent architecture coordinates condition analysis, knowledge retrieval, controller composition, review,knowledge-base management, and experience write-back. Three agents are LLM-backed and operateonly at the high-level design stage; low-level power commands remain with executable controlmodules. Mission scenarios were constructed using a public AUV navigation dataset, while theplant’s source-power envelopes were calibrated against prototype measurements. Across the evaluatedmissions, KACG generated controllers that outperformed all 15 reference controllers, includingadvanced EMS methods. Under the complex mission, KACG achieved a composite score 32.7% lowerthan that of the strongest reference controller. Applied separately to four single-condition missions,the generated controller ranked first in every case, with an average score reduction of 10.6% relative tothe strongest controller in each condition. Knowledge-base guidance further reduced offline controller-generation time from 106.8 to 21.4 minutes. These results show that KACG can automatically designhigh-performing controllers for changing mission conditions within the evaluated plant and missionscope.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7140979`

- title: The Harms of Large Language Model Misuse in the Creation of Destructive Capabilities: A Defensive Review of CBRN, Cyber, and Harmful-Content Risks
- authors: Rafi Rahman
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-03
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7140979
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have intensified a long-running debate over whether they meaningfully lower the barrier for actors seeking to build destructive capabilities across chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear (CBRN) weapons, offensive cyber operations, and the mass production of harmful or hateful content. This paper is a structured narrative review of the empirical evidence published between 2024 and early 2026. It argues that the resulting harm is real but uneven, and that it is best understood through the concept of marginal uplift, meaning the improvement a tool provides over a realistic baseline, rather than through raw model capability. Drawing on randomized red-team studies, developer-disclosed uplift trials, provider threat-intelligence reporting, and primary technical literature, the review reaches a domain-differentiated conclusion. For CBRN, current models have not been shown to provide reliable novel capability beyond existing resources, although disclosed uplift trials indicate a narrowing margin with each model generation. For offensive cyber and harmful-content generation, the uplift is more tangible and accelerating, principally by compressing time, lowering the skill floor, and industrializing volume. Recent criminal and civil cases further show conversational models being consulted in the commission of lethal violence, chiefly in operational and psychological roles rather than as sources of novel capability. The paper examines the principal defenses and their documented failure modes, contrasts the divergent governance approaches of the European Union and the United States as of mid-2026, and concludes that the dominant near-term harm is a model acting as a force multiplier on existing harmful knowledge rather than inventing new weapons, but a model acting as a force multiplier that renders existing harmful knowledge faster, cheaper, and more broadly accessible.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7210480`

- title: Keeping AI on Track: A Drift Detection Service for Monitoring Context Windows in Production
- authors: Lei Chang
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-03
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7210480
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

The deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) in production software introduces a critical failure mode distinct from traditional system outages: the gradual, often invisible degradation of performance as the model's context window accumulates information. This paper introduces Drift Detection as a Service (DDaaS), a novel framework for continuously monitoring the semantic and structural integrity of AI context windows in real-world applications. While conventional observability tools track latency and throughput, they fail to capture the cognitive decay that manifests as context rot, semantic drift, and temporal blindness. Drawing on empirical research establishing that LLM performance degrades predictably as context windows approach capacity-with retrieval accuracy dropping for information in the middle of the context and models exhibiting "lost-in-the-middle" effectswe propose a service-oriented architecture that operationalizes drift detection through multiple complementary signals. Our approach integrates several detection modalities: statistical analysis of domain alignment distributions to identify flattening bell curves that signal structural degradation; embedding-based semantic drift monitoring using cosine similarity against baseline representations; and model-specific degradation curves calibrated to token utilization thresholds. The framework further incorporates phase-aware classification that distinguishes between coherent, reorganizing, and fragmented cognitive states, enabling context management actions-compression, summarization, or session handoff-only when the model enters a degraded phase. We demonstrate that drift detection must be delivered as a service integrated into the AI development lifecycle, providing real-time health scoring, actionable recovery recommendations, and automatic rollback triggers for safety-critical metrics. The service architecture supports production-grade requirements including telemetry pipelines, observability integration, and policy-based intervention gates that prevent promotion of new configurations while drift is unresolved. Our findings establish that effective drift detection requires moving beyond output-based evaluation to continuous monitoring of the context window itself-a shift from asking "is the system running?" to "is the system reasoning correctly?" The DDaaS framework provides the foundational infrastructure for this new paradigm of AI system reliability.

## uid: `arxiv:2608.02684v2`

- title: A Blind Spot in Alignment: Quantifying Biosecurity Risks in Large Language Models
- authors: Shu Quan, Tianfang Hao, Sitong Fang, He Geng, Jiayi Zhou, Boyuan Chen, Kaile Wang, Donghai Hong
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-03
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02684v2
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are accelerating biological research, yet this same capability poses a critical biosecurity threat: models that assist in protein engineering can equally be prompted to generate predicted toxin-like sequences, potentially lowering the barrier to biological misuse. Current safety evaluations, however, operate in natural language and cannot determine whether a model-generated amino acid sequence is biological gibberish or a computational risk signal. To address this evaluation blind spot, we introduce SPIKE-Bench, coupling 631 curated toxin-design prompts across seven functional categories with the SPIKE funnel, a three-stage protocol that filters output through compliance, biological plausibility, and predicted toxicity, producing stage-level diagnostics and an aggregate function-aware metric: the Functional Harmfulness Rate (FHR). An audit of 32 LLMs reveals that most models freely comply with toxin-design requests; FHR is driven primarily by biological generation capability rather than safety alignment, reaching 50.7%; and Refusal Rate fails to predict functional risk. As a first step toward mitigation, we provide BioSafe-Guard, a domain-specialized classifier that substantially reduces predicted functional risk while preserving benign utility. We release SPIKE-Bench and BioSafe-Guard at https://github.com/PKU-Alignment/SPIKE-Bench to support more rigorous biosecurity evaluation of LLMs.

## uid: `arxiv:2608.01629v1`

- title: Human-LLM Alignment in Language Attitudes Toward Non-Native Japanese
- authors: Naho Orita, Hayato Ogawa, Daisuke Kawahara
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-03
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01629v1
- keyword hits: large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Large language models (LLMs) increasingly evaluate human writing in high-stakes domains such as hiring and academic assessment, putting non-native speakers at particular risk. Drawing on the language attitudes framework, we compared human and LLM evaluations of parallel L1- and L2-written Japanese emails on three dimensions: fluency, status, and solidarity. Japanese raters rated L2 texts significantly lower on all three dimensions, with a fluency gap roughly twice the size of the status and solidarity gaps. Six LLM judges reproduced the direction of this bias, and five reproduced its ordering across dimensions. The models diverged from humans in two ways: all understated the solidarity gap, the most socially grounded dimension, and all differentiated among learner L1 backgrounds where humans did not. LLM judges thus reproduce native speakers' language attitudes in a structured yet attenuated form, and the language attitudes framework offers a ready-made yardstick for auditing them beyond English.
