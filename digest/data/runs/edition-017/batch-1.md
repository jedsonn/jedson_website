## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.6644227`

- title: Stochastic Parrots or Singing in Harmony? Testing Five Leading LLMs for their Ability to Replicate a Human Survey with Synthetic Data
- authors: Jason Miklian, Kristian Hoelscher, John Katsos
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-04-25
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6644227
- keyword hits: ai agent, chatgpt, claude, deepseek, gemini, generative ai, large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

How well can AI-derived synthetic research data replicate the responses of human participants? This article presents a comparison between a human-respondent survey of 420 Silicon Valley coders and developers and "synthetic" survey data designed to simulate real organizational survey takers generated by five leading Generative AI Large Language Models (LLMs): ChatGPT Thinking 5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5 Pro plus Claude Code / CoWork 1.123, Gemini Advanced 2.5 Pro, Incredible 1.0, and DeepSeek 3.2. An emerging literature has begun to engage with this question, which carries deep implications for innovation and science policy research practice. Our findings reveal that while AI agents produced technically plausible results that lean more towards replicability and harmonization than typically assumed, none of the LLMs were able to capture the counterintuitive insights of the human survey. Moreover, this pattern was repeated across different prompt formations, such that deviations in synthetic data grouped together for all models, leaving the real data as the apparent ‘outlier’. Our key methodological finding is that while leading LLMs are increasingly being used to scale, replicate and replace human survey responses in innovation and science policy research, these advances only illustrate an increased capacity to parrot conventional wisdom in harmony with each other rather than revealing novel findings. If synthetic respondents are used in future research, we need more replicable validation protocols and reporting standards for when and where synthetic survey data can be used responsibly in innovation and science policy research, a gap that this paper helps fill. Our results suggest that synthetic survey responses cannot meaningfully model real human social beliefs, particularly in contexts lacking previously documented evidence. We conclude that synthetic survey-based research should be cast not as a substitute for rigorous survey methods, but as an increasingly reliable data collection instrument for identifying societal assumptions, conventional wisdoms, and other expectations about research populations within organizations.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7199289`

- title: Multi-Layer Semantic Integrity Framework for Knowledge-Reliable and Consistent Responses in Large Language Models
- authors: Vadivel Abishethvarman, Fariza Sabrina, Paul Kwan
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-03
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7199289
- keyword hits: deepseek, large language model, large language models, llama, llm, llms, mistral, qwen

### abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate responses that appear fluent yet may contain hallucinations, inconsistencies, or biased reasoning, that can pose significant integrity risks for knowledge-based applications. This paper proposes a Multi-Layer Semantic Integrity Framework (MLSIF) that automatically evaluates the integrity of LLM responses based on six complementary metrics: response consistency, semantic similarity, reasoning quality, adversarial robustness, bias estimation, and perplexity. These metrics are combined into a novel Semantic Integrity Index (SII) that provides a unified interpretable reliability score per model. The framework is evaluated on eight open-source LLMs including Gemma2 9B, DeepSeek-R1 7B, LLaMA2 7B, LLaMA3.1 8B, LLaMA3.2 3B, Qwen2.5 7B, Mistral 7B, and Vicuna 7B using 200 structured prompts that span five knowledge categories, namely factual, reasoning, contextual, adversarial, and sensitive. Experimental results reveal that LLaMA3.2 3B achieves the highest SII (0.688) despite being the smallest model, demonstrating that parameter count alone does not imply semantic reliability. DeepSeek-R1 7B leads in reasoning quality (mean = 0.600), while Mistral 7B achieves the highest adversarial robustness (mean = 0.809). ANOVA and Tukey HSD analysis confirm statistically significant differences between the tested models on response consistency, reasoning quality, adversarial robustness, and bias estimation. Noteworthy, the proposed framework is model-agnostic and extensible, providing a principled pre-deployment validation mechanism for LLM’s integration into knowledge-based systems.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7216687`

- title: An Agentic System for Autonomous Surface Vessel Navigation
- authors: Arjun Silwal, Rahul Dubey
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-02
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7216687
- keyword hits: agentic, chatgpt, claude, deepseek, gpt-5, large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

Autonomous vessels must follow the same collision-avoidance rules as crewed vessels while ensuring their decisions can be verified. Although several navigation methods have been proposed, they often lack reliability, robustness, scalability, or full compliance with the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs), whose interpretation frequently requires expert judgment. Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) enable natural-language reasoning about maritime situations, but their performance depends heavily on prompt design and they remain susceptible to hallucinations. To address these challenges, this paper presents the Maritime Agentic Reasoning System (MARS-AGENT), a navigation framework that combines an LLM planner with a deterministic COLREGs guard. The system converts situational information into prompts, generates candidate maneuvers, and validates both the reasoning and proposed actions before execution. We evaluated MARS-AGENT across eight maritime scenarios using three state-of-the-art LLMs: ChatGPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.6, and DeepSeek. Results show that all executed maneuvers remained COLREGs-compliant while achieving mission objectives in over 80% of trials with a high level of safety. Additional experiments demonstrate the framework's reliability and robustness. The proposed architecture provides a practical blueprint for trustworthy agentic navigation and can be extended to other autonomous platforms, including unmanned underwater and aerial vehicles.

- title: Knowledge or Correlation? How Large Language Models ChooseFeatures
- authors: Sara Rivera-Méndez, Johanna  C. Willis-Ruiz, Gilberto Ochoa-Ruiz, Enrique García-Ceja, Salvador Miguel Hinojosa-Cervantes
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-04
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7228080
- keyword hits: deepseek, gpt-3, gpt-4, large language model, large language models, llama, llm, llms

### abstract

Feature selection is foundational to supervised learning and to intelligent decision-support systems, where a variable’s informativeness is often encoded in human-readable metadata. A common claim is that large language models (LLMs) can act as zero-shot feature selectors that exploit the domain knowledge encoded in feature names. We test whether LLMs use that encoded knowledge, or merely the statistical signal presented alongside it, in an iterative classification framework that adds the next feature given the task description, the selected feature names, validation accuracy, and each candidate’s Pearson correlation with the class label (𝜌).Two converging lines of evidence indicate that knowledge utilization does not drive the framework. First, on synthetic datasets whose informative features have |𝜌| ≈ 0 but high mutual information, all four LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-3.5 Turbo, Llama-4, DeepSeek-R1) achieve 0% selection precision while reaching 84–100% on linearly correlated controls; an anonymisation ablation that strips meaning from feature names changes accuracy negligibly. Second, a non-LLM forward-selection-with-feedback wrapper consuming the same per-iteration signal outperforms every LLM, winning 150 of 200 paired runs. The gains over a greedy correlation baseline, therefore, come from the iterative protocol, not the LLM.We characterize the LLM as a stochastic greedy policy over the supplied correlation column;interventions on the semantics, richness, and prompt-position of feature names leave its selections unchanged while a correlation signal is present, and names regain influence only when it is withheld.The framework retains value as a re-targetable, anonymisation-robust selector when an exhaustive wrapper search is infeasible, and we give usage guidance.

- title: Energy- and Memory-Efficient PEFT Methods for Personalized On-Device SLMs on Consumer GPUs
- authors: Kuanysh Akhmetzhanov, Jurn-Gyu Park
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-05
- source: arXiv
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04488v1
- keyword hits: fine-tuning, large language model, large language models, llama, llm, llms, qwen, transformer model

### abstract

Despite rapid advances in large language models (LLMs), deploying and personalizing them on resource-constrained devices remains impractical due to high VRAM, time, and energy costs. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) of Small Language Models (SLMs) offers a promising alternative, yet few studies compare PEFT methods across architectures using both general and personalization benchmarks while accounting for energy consumption. We compare five fine-tuning approaches (Full Fine-Tuning, LoRA, LoRA+, QLoRA, and BitFit) on four SLMs from two families (Transformer-based: TinyLlama-1.1B, Qwen3-1.7B; SSM-based: Mamba-1.4B, Mamba-2-1.3B) across three GLUE tasks (SST-2, QNLI, STS-B) and three LaMP personalization tasks (LaMP-1, LaMP-2, LaMP-3). Each configuration is evaluated with the energy-focused NetScore-E and the memory-focused NetScore-M, the two variants that reflect the constraints binding on-device deployment. Methods are selected with a strict energy-first rule (highest NetScore-E, ties broken by NetScore#). LoRA+ achieves the highest NetScore-E in 19 of 24 configurations and the highest NetScore-M in 13 of 24, and is the selected method in 18 of 24. QLoRA, available only for the Transformer models, cuts peak finetuning VRAM by up to 3.9x relative to LoRA and therefore takes the best NetScore-M in 5 of the 12 Transformer configurations, although its de-quantization overhead leaves it selected in only one of them once energy decides. BitFit and full fine-tuning are almost never competitive on either variant, and TinyLlama-1.1B leads the energy-focused NetScore-E on five of the six benchmarks and the memory-focused NetScore-M on four. These results show that compact SLMs paired with PEFT provide a practical, energy-aware path to personalized on-device deployment, with the optimal method set by the dominant constraint: LoRA+ for energy and QLoRA for memory.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7193558`

- title: Evaluating AI-Generated Code Attribution Accuracy Across Leading LLMs Using a Session-Based Fingerprinting Protocol
- authors: Michael Harris
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-06
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7193558
- keyword hits: claude, gemini, gpt-4, large language model, large language models, llm, llms

### abstract

The widespread integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into software development has introduced a critical transparency challenge: version control systems currently lack reliable mechanisms to attribute code contributions to the specific AI session that generated them. This ambiguity complicates debugging, security auditing, and compliance with emerging AI governance frameworks. To address this gap, we propose and evaluate a session-based fingerprinting protocol that captures behavioral and syntactic traces-including typing latency patterns, intermediate variable renaming sequences, and edit-distance trajectories-during human-AI collaborative coding sessions. We conducted a controlled empirical study using 4,800 coding tasks completed by 40 professional developers across three leading LLMs (GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini Pro). Each session was instrumented with our fingerprinting protocol, and we measured attribution accuracy via a blind classification task where a random forest classifier attempted to match anonymized commit fragments back to their originating session. Our results demonstrate a mean attribution accuracy of 87.3% (F1=0.84) across all models, with significantly higher accuracy for longer sessions (>15 minutes) and lower accuracy when sessions involved aggressive post-hoc refactoring. We further analyze failure modes, identifying session fragmentation and multi-turn context resets as primary sources of misattribution. These findings establish that deterministic session attribution is technically feasible without modifying LLM internals, and they provide a benchmark for future transparency tooling. We conclude by discussing the protocol's integration with existing Git workflows and its limitations under collaborative pairprogramming scenarios.

## uid: `doi:10.2139/ssrn.7226272`

- title: Write-Time Conflict Governance for TrustworthyDynamic RAG:An Auditable Temporal-Confidence DominanceProtocol
- authors: Yishan Liu, Huimin Qian, Fangshu Cao
- affiliations: not stated
- posted: 2026-08-03
- source: SSRN
- link: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7226272
- keyword hits: claude, deepseek, gpt-5, llm, llms, qwen, retrieval-augmented

### abstract

Dynamic knowledge graphs powering retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems ingest triples from heterogeneous, continuously-updated sources. The dominant write policy, last-write-wins (LWW), is unsafe under adversarial injection: any late-arriving write silently overwrites a trusted fact regardless of its provenance, collapsing retrieval accuracy to near zero under attack. We propose TRC, an auditable write-time ingestion layer that resolves temporal knowledge conflicts through a two-level dominance rule: prefer the more recent fact when timestamps are sufficiently separated; block same-time writes whose confidence margin falls below a threshold; escalate only rare unresolved ties to an optional LLM arbiter. Every decision is recorded as a structured certificate, making the active graph traceable and replayable without query-time overhead. We also introduce CN-TempLAMA, a Wikidata-derived Chinese temporal benchmark (196,435 rows; 2010–2024) aligned with English TempLAMA, with 100% most-recent answer coverage and zero empty-answer rate. Experiments show TRC preserves clean-update fidelity while LWW collapses to ≤0.017 exact match (EM) across 9 state-of-the-art LLMs (including GPT-5.4-mini, Claude-Haiku-4.5, and DeepSeek-R1) and 18 model–language evaluation cells spanning 5 providers. TRC reaches 0.28–0.96 EM, with 12 of 18 cells exceeding 0.85. A six-LLM comparison with RobustRAG-style read-time defenses reveals four provider-specific profiles: substitute (OpenAI), partial substitute (Google), complement (Anthropic), and regression (Qwen, 82–99% abstention). Write-time TRC is the only governance configuration portable across all four provider families at minimum prompt-token cost.
